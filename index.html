Polygons in contact

This is Hankin's method, published by E. H. Hankin in 1925 after studying Islamic architectural ornament. Start from a tiling, then from the midpoint of every edge send two rays into each tile. Each ray stops where it first meets another ray, and the stopped rays are the pattern.

The contact angle

θ is the angle between a ray and its edge. For a tile with n sides, the critical angle is 180°/n. Below it, rays from neighbouring edges meet near the corner and the design hugs the tiling. Above it, rays reach deeper and the star points sharpen. The chips under the pattern show where you are for each tile shape.

The offset

δ slides the two rays apart along the edge, as a fraction of the edge length. Positive values make them cross just inside the edge, which reads as two strands woven through. Negative values split them into parallel pairs, the start of a doubled-line style.

Symmetry

The pattern keeps every symmetry of the tiling underneath it. Square and 4.8.8 tilings give wallpaper group p4m (fourfold turns and mirrors). The triangle and hexagon families give p6m, with sixfold turns. Try counting the mirror lines in a single star.
